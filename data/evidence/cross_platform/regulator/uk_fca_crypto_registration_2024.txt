Citation: UK Financial Conduct Authority, "Cryptoassets: AML/CTF regime" and "Cryptoassets: Who needs to register." Implemented under The Money Laundering, Terrorist Financing and Transfer of Funds (Information on the Payer) Regulations 2017 (MLRs), as amended.

KEY TEXT:

Effective from: January 10, 2020 (MLRs amendments), with ongoing FCA supervision.

Scope: Cryptoasset businesses (CABs) must register with the FCA under Regulation 14A of the MLRs if they intend to provide certain cryptoasset services by way of business in the UK. In-scope services include:
- Cryptoasset exchange providers (fiat <-> crypto, crypto <-> crypto, peer-to-peer matching).
- Custodian wallet providers (safekeeping or administering crypto on behalf of customers).
- ATMs (cryptoasset ATM operators).
- ICO issuers offering services in the UK.

Application requirements:
- Money Laundering Reporting Officer (MLRO) / Nominated Officer with appropriate UK regulation knowledge, cryptoasset experience, training, authority, and independence.
- Customer Due Diligence (CDD) and Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD) programs.
- Transaction monitoring program.
- Suspicious Activity Reporting via the UK National Crime Agency (NCA).
- Compliance with sanctions screening (UK HM Treasury OFSI list).

Travel Rule (in-force from 1 September 2023): UK adopted FATF Travel Rule via Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing (Amendment) Regulations 2022. Cryptoasset transfers over £1,000 must include originator and beneficiary identifying information transmitted between VASPs.

Supervision approach: Risk-based; firms posing greater ML/TF risk receive enhanced supervisory focus.

Registration statistics (FCA published data): As of 2024, the FCA had registered approximately 50 cryptoasset firms; over 290 applications were withdrawn or rejected — indicating significant compliance friction.

Future regime: Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 (Cryptoassets) Regulations expected in force October 2027 (per UK Treasury announcement).
