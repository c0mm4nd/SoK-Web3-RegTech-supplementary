Citation: Regulation (EU) 2023/1114 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 31 May 2023 on markets in crypto-assets ("MiCA"), and amending Regulations (EU) No 1093/2010 and (EU) No 1095/2010 and Directives 2013/36/EU and (EU) 2019/1937. Official Journal of the European Union L 150/40, 9 June 2023.

KEY TEXT:

Publication date: 9 June 2023 (OJ L 150/40); Entry into force: 29 June 2023.

Application dates:
- Provisions on Asset-Referenced Tokens (ARTs) and E-Money Tokens (EMTs): from 30 June 2024.
- All other provisions (including CASP authorization): from 30 December 2024.

Scope: Establishes uniform EU rules for crypto-assets that are not currently regulated by existing EU financial-services legislation. Covers three categories:
1. Asset-Referenced Tokens (ARTs): tokens aiming to maintain stable value by referencing one or more fiat currencies, commodities, or crypto-assets.
2. E-Money Tokens (EMTs): tokens purporting to maintain stable value by referencing one official currency.
3. Other Crypto-Assets: utility tokens and other crypto-assets not classified as ARTs or EMTs.

Key obligations:
- Issuers of ARTs and EMTs must publish a crypto-asset white paper notified to competent national authorities.
- Crypto-Asset Service Providers (CASPs) — including custody, exchange, trading, advice — must be authorized by an EU competent authority. CASPs include any entity offering services to clients in the EU.
- Market-abuse rules (insider dealing, unlawful disclosure, market manipulation) apply to all crypto-assets admitted to trading.
- Stablecoin issuers subject to reserve requirements (1:1 backing of EMTs; liquidity/reserve requirements for ARTs).
- Transparency, disclosure, governance, and prudential safeguards mandated for CASPs.

Cross-references:
- Travel Rule equivalent: Regulation (EU) 2023/1113 (Transfer of Funds Regulation, revised) — applies FATF Travel Rule to crypto transfers within EU.
- Anti-Money Laundering: forthcoming AMLA (Anti-Money Laundering Authority Regulation 2024) supplements MiCA.
