ABSTRACT (near-verbatim from publisher description / Bournemouth Univ. eprint):

"With the advance of Bitcoin technology, money laundering has been incentivized in the Bitcoin blockchain where user identity is hidden behind a pseudonym known as 'address'. Although this permits concealing in plain sight, the public ledger of Bitcoin blockchain provides more power for investigators and allows collective intelligence for anti-money laundering and forensic analysis. In this paper, we apply classical supervised learning models, namely Logistic Regression, Random Forest, Multi-Layer Perceptron, and an ensemble learning approach combining Random Forest with Multi-Layer Perceptron, on a time series of Bitcoin transaction graph published by Elliptic, an emerging crypto-currency intelligence company. Our main contribution points out that using ensemble learning approach outperforms the performance of classical learning models, using the Elliptic dataset, a time series of Bitcoin transaction graph with 49 distinct time steps."
