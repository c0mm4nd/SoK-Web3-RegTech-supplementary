KEY TEXT (verbatim/near-verbatim quotes from DOJ release):

Date of action: November 21, 2023.

Defendant entities: Binance Holdings Limited (d/b/a Binance.com); Changpeng Zhao (founder and CEO).

Charges pleaded by Binance:
- Conspiracy to conduct an unlicensed money transmitting business in violation of 18 U.S.C. Section 1960
- Conspiracy to violate the Bank Secrecy Act (BSA) by failing to implement and maintain an effective anti-money laundering program (31 U.S.C. Section 5318(h))
- Failure to register as a money transmitting business
- Violations of the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA) for willful sanctions violations

Charges pleaded by Changpeng Zhao: One count of failing to maintain an effective AML program under the BSA (18 U.S.C. Section 1960 / 31 U.S.C. Section 5318(h)). Personally paid $50 million.

Total penalties: Over $4.3 billion across DOJ, OFAC, FinCEN, and CFTC components — among the largest corporate criminal resolutions in U.S. history.

Compliance remediation:
- Five-year monitorship by an independent compliance monitor selected by FinCEN and OFAC.
- Required compliance enhancements including: full transaction monitoring program, customer identification program, BSA/AML compliance officer, complete review of historical transactions for SAR-filing obligations, ongoing engagement with U.S. regulators.
- Required to exit the U.S. market entirely.

Key AML failures cited:
- Did not implement KYC for years; allowed customers to register with only an email address.
- Did not file Suspicious Activity Reports (SARs) on transactions involving sanctioned jurisdictions (Iran, Cuba, Syria, Crimea), known terror-finance entities (Hamas, al-Qaeda, ISIS), or ransomware operators.
- Knowingly failed to screen transactions with users in OFAC-sanctioned jurisdictions.
