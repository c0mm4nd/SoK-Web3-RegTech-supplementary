# Overlap Analysis (v2, evidence-anchored)

This note records, after the eight-case v2 mapping in `data/cases/`, where the three SoK taxonomies of `3_systematization.tex` overlap in practice, and whether those overlaps are taxonomy defects or genuine multi-faceted phenomena. We frame the discussion against Nickerson et al. (2013), *A method for taxonomy development and its application in information systems*, which holds that a good taxonomy should be (i) **mutually exclusive** within each dimension, (ii) **collectively exhaustive**, and (iii) **useful** — i.e., it should produce distinctions that matter for the question at hand. Every overlap claim below cites a specific retrieved evidence file under `data/evidence/`.

## Where the overlaps occur

**Taxonomy 1 (Regulatory Paradigm).** Eight of eight cases admit a single column with no ambiguity. The boundary case is Circle USDC freezing (Case 04): the policy actor is a Centralized VASP, but the enforcement substrate is a public smart contract. Allaire's verbatim statement (`data/evidence/04_circle_usdc_freeze/circle_allaire_tornado_cash_response.txt`: "Centre, the consortium behind USDC, followed the US Treasury sanctions against Tornado Cash and blocked the related addresses") makes clear the *actor* is the centralized consortium and the trigger is a traditional sanctions order; the contract is the *substrate*. This matches the row "Enforcement Mechanism: Similar to traditional plus blockchain address blacklisting" in `tab:regulatory_evolution`. The taxonomy survives because the rows are about *who acts and how*, not about *where the bytes execute*.

**Taxonomy 2 (Compliance Mechanism).** Five of eight cases have a real and non-spurious secondary mechanism: Tornado Cash sanctions (Screening + KYT — `data/evidence/01_tornado_cash/chainalysis_tornado_cash_sanctions.txt`), Ronin (KYT + Screening — `data/evidence/02_ronin_bridge/merkle_science_ronin_analysis.txt`), Notabene (KYC + KYB, with KYA tertiary — `data/evidence/05_notabene_travel_rule/notabene_state_of_travel_rule_2024.txt`), Polygon ID (KYA + KYC — `data/evidence/06_polygon_id/polygon_blog_intro_mar2022.txt` names "KYC, KYB, attestation" together), and DOJ Binance (KYC + KYT — `data/evidence/07_doj_binance/cftc_v_binance_mar2023.txt`). In every case a *single* mechanism is dominant and the others are derivative (e.g., Tornado Cash KYT only fires because of the Screening list). We therefore treat the secondary column not as a taxonomy failure but as evidence that the five mechanisms compose: Screening is downstream of KYT, KYA is downstream of KYC. This is consistent with the two-layer framework figure already in the manuscript.

**Taxonomy 3 (RegTech Lifecycle).** Six of eight cases sit cleanly in a single phase (secondary == `None`). The two that genuinely straddle phases are:

1. **Ronin (Case 02).** Ex-Post forensics produced an In-Medias-Res screening control via the OFAC SDN listing on April 14 2022 (`data/evidence/02_ronin_bridge/ofac_lazarus_ronin_apr2022.txt`). The Halborn write-up (`data/evidence/02_ronin_bridge/halborn_ronin_postmortem.txt`) records a six-day detection lag (Ex-Post), while the Merkle Science blog (`data/evidence/02_ronin_bridge/merkle_science_ronin_analysis.txt`) shows the SDN listing converted into a screening input thereafter.
2. **Notabene (Case 05).** In-Medias-Res Travel Rule exchange (`data/evidence/05_notabene_travel_rule/fatf_updated_guidance_vasp_oct2021.txt`: "transfer originator and beneficiary information … above the de-minimis threshold") with an Ex-Ante pre-transaction counterparty due-diligence step (`data/evidence/05_notabene_travel_rule/notabene_state_of_travel_rule_2024.txt`: "66% of VASPs enforce restrictions on withdrawals to self-hosted … wallets").

Both overlaps are explicit in the original tables: the Ex-Post row lists "cross-chain fund tracing" while the In-Medias-Res row lists "automated Travel Rule protocols", and these are different *activities* even though one case may exercise both.

## Are overlaps taxonomy defects?

Applying Nickerson et al.'s criteria:

- **Exclusivity.** On the lifecycle dimension we observe 6/8 mutually exclusive assignments; the two non-exclusive cases (Ronin, Notabene) span phases for a *substantive* reason rather than because the taxonomy is incoherent. The mechanism dimension has 0/8 mutually exclusive primary-only assignments (every case has a meaningful secondary) — but this is because mechanisms compose, not because they overlap. We therefore treat exclusivity as approximately satisfied on lifecycle and *non-applicable* on mechanism: the taxonomy classifies technologies, while cases bundle technologies.
- **Exhaustiveness.** All eight cases were assignable. We did not encounter a case that required inventing a new column on any of the three taxonomies. The closest near-miss is Polygon ID (Case 06), which is an identity substrate rather than a deployed regime; assigning it required reading the substrate as an Ex-Ante intervention rather than a "no phase" outlier. This is weak evidence for exhaustiveness given the small N, but no counter-example arose.
- **Utility.** The mapping produced non-trivial distinctions: it separated centralized-issuer compliance (Cases 04, 07) from protocol-substrate compliance (Cases 01, 06), separated forensics (Cases 02, 03, 08) from real-time gating (Case 05), and surfaced the Ronin overlap as a genuinely informative cross-phase case rather than a definitional accident. The v2 distribution (4 Web3 / 4 VASP; 3 Ex-Ante / 4 Ex-Post / 1 In-Medias-Res) is balanced enough to differentiate cases on each axis.

## Conclusion

The overlaps observed in this exercise look like genuine multi-faceted phenomena, not taxonomy defects. We recommend that the manuscript (a) keep the three taxonomies as defined, (b) explicitly acknowledge in the appendix that lifecycle overlaps occur for incident-response cases that produce post-hoc sanctions controls (Ronin pattern), and (c) note that the secondary-mechanism column is *expected* to be populated because the five compliance mechanisms compose by design.
